Alejandro (Alex) was born in <em>La Plata</em>, <em>Argentina</em>. In 1991, he moved to <em>Barcelona</em>, <em>Spain</em>, where he obtained his undergraduate degree in <b>Telecommunications Engineering</b> from the <em>Technical University of Catalonia (Barcelona)</em> in 1996. Then he carried out research on electrical impedance tomography for image reconstruction and noise characterization at the same institution under a <em>CIRIT</em> grant. In 1997 he obtained a grant from the <em>Dutch Ministry of Economic Affairs</em> to pursue his <b>PhD in Medicine</b> at the <em>Image Sciences Institute of the University Medical Center Utrecht</em> on model-based cardiovascular image analysis. During this period he was visiting researcher at the <em>Imperial College</em> in <em>London, UK</em>, and at <em>Philips Medical Systems BV, The Netherlands</em>.

Prof Frangi is the <b>Bicentennial Turing Chair in Computational Medicine</b> at the <em>University of Manchester, Manchester, UK</em>, with joint appointments at the <em>Schools of Computer Science</em> and <em>Health Science</em>. He is also the <b>Royal Academy of Engineering Chair in Emerging Technologies</b>, with a focus on <em>Precision Computational Medicine</em> for in silico trials of medical devices. His research vision was recently awarded an <b>Advanced Grant</b> from the <em>European Research Council</em> under the <em>Computer Science and Informatics</em> (PE6) panel. He is an <b>Alan Turing Institute Fellow</b>. Since 2001, he has founded and directed the [Centre for Computational Imaging and Simulation Technologies in Biomedicine](https://cistib.org/). Prof Frangi has visiting positions at <em>KU Leuven</em>, the <em>Cixi Institute of Biomedical Engineering</em>, <em>CNITECH Chinese Academy of Science</em> (Ningbo), the <em>Zhejiang University of Technology</em> (Hangzhou), <em>Shenzhen University</em> (Shenzhen), and <em>Beijing Institute of Technology</em> (Beijing).

At the national level, Prof Frangi leads [InSilicoUK Innovation Network](http://www.insilicouk.org/), spearheading in silico methods as a source of regulatory evidence and accelerated innovation in the life sciences sector. Prof Frangi is among the <b>top 90 Computer Scientists</b> in the UK, has edited several books, published seven editorial articles and more than 285 journal articles in key international journals of his research field, and more than 200 book chapters and international conference articles with an <b>h-index of 83</b> and <b>more than 39400 citations</b> according to [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=9fGrB0sAAAAJ&hl=en&oi=ao). He has been three times Guest Editor of special issues of <em>IEEE Trans Med Imaging</em>, one of <em>IEEE Trans Biomed Eng</em>, and one of <em>Medical Image Analysis</em> journals. He was chair of the <em>Third International Conference on Functional Imaging and Modelling of the Heart</em> (FIMH05) held in <em>Barcelona</em> in June 2005, <em>Publications Chair</em> of the <em>IEEE International Symposium in Biomedical Imaging</em> (ISBI 2006), <em>Programme Committee Member</em> of various editions of the <em>Intl Conf on Medical Image Computing and Computer Assisted Interventions</em> (MICCAI) (<em>Brisbane, AU</em>, 2007; <em>Beijing, CN</em>, 2010; <em>Toronto CA</em>, 2011; <em>Nice, FR</em> 2012; <em>Nagoya, JP</em>, 2013), <em>International Liaison of ISBI 2009</em>, <em>Tutorials Co-Chair of MICCAI 2010</em>, and <em>Programme Co-chair of MICCAI 2015</em>. He was also the <em>General Chair for ISBI 2012</em>, held in <em>Barcelona</em>, <em>MICCAI 2018</em>, held in <em>Granada, Spain</em>, and <em>Information Processing in Medical Imaging</em> (IPMI) <em>2023</em> in <em>Bariloche, Argentina</em>.

Prof Frangi is <em>Chair of the Editorial Board of the MICCAI-Elsevier Book Series</em> (2017-2020), is <em>Associate Editor-in-Chief of Progress in Bioengineering</em> (IOP Press), and serves as <em>Associate Editor of IEEE Trans on Medical Imaging</em>, <em>Medical Image Analysis</em>, <em>SIAM Journal Imaging Sciences</em>, <em>Computer Vision and Image Understanding</em> journals. Prof Frangi was a foreign member of the <b>Review College of the Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council</b> (EPSRC, 2006-10) in the UK. He is a recipient of the <b>IEEE Engineering in Medicine and Biology Technical Achievement (2021) and Early Career (2006) Awards</b>, the <em>ICT Knowledge Transfer Prize</em> (2008) and two <em>Teaching Excellence Prizes</em> (2008 and 2010) by the <em>Social Council of the Universitat Pompeu Fabra</em>. He also received the <b>UPF Medal</b> (2011) for his service as <b>Dean of the Escuela Politècnica Superior</b>. He was awarded the <em>ICREA-Academia Prize</em> by the <em>Institució Catalana de Recerca i Estudis Avançats</em> (ICREA) in 2008, a <b>President's International Initiative Award</b> in 2019 by the <b>Chinese Academy of Science</b>, and a <em>Pengcheng Scholar Programme Distinguished Professor</em> at <em>Shenzhen University</em> by the <em>Shenzhen Education Bureau</em>. 

Prof Frangi is an <b>IEEE Fellow</b> (2014), <b>EAMBES Fellow</b> (2013), <b>SPIE Fellow</b> (2020), <b>AAIA-AI Fellow</b> (2021), <b>MICCAI Fellow</b> (2021), <b>ATI Fellow</b> (2021), <b>MICCAI Fellow</b> (2021), and <b>SIAM</b> and <b>IET Member</b>. He served as an elected member of the <b>Board of Directors of the Medical Image Computing and Computer-Assisted Interventions Society</b> (MICCAI) (2014-2019). Prof. Frangi served on the <em>Scientific Advisory Board</em> of the <em>European Institute for Biomedical Imaging Research</em> (EIBIR, 2014-2018) and Chaired the <em>Fellows Committee of the IEEE Engineering in Medicine and Biology Society</em> (2017-2018). Under his leadership, <b>CISTIB</b> developed <em>GIMIAS (Graphical Interface for Medical Image Analysis and Simulation)</em>, an open-source platform to rapidly develop pre-commercial software prototypes in the areas of image computing and image-based computational physiology modelling, and <em>MULTI-X (Health Data Analytics and Modelling As a Service Platform)</em>, a cloud-based platform for computational phenomics, in silico medicine, and in silico clinical trials. Research and development carried out in his group led to three spin-off companies (<em>Clintelis SA</em> 2009, <em>[GalgoMedical SA](http://www.galgomedical.com/)</em> 2013 and <em>[adsilico Ltd](https://adsilico.uk/)</em> 2022).

## student education

At the moment I do not have teaching commitments at the undergraduate level but am <b>intensively supervising MSc and PhD students at the School of Computer Science and at the School of Health Sciences</b> in the areas of <em>computational imaging and computational biomechanics</em> with a particular interest in <em>cardiovascular, metabolic and bone disease</em> and in application to <em>in silico trials of medical devices</em>. See the [InSilico UK](https://www.insilicouk.org/) Network I lead for more details. 

I am interested in working with motivated students to <b>solve unmet clinical needs in medicine by utilising novel computational medicine methods</b>. I work both with students willing to <b>develop fundamental algorithms in computational imaging, machine learning and computational mechanics</b> as well as with students seeking to <b>translate these methods to the clinical setting</b>. 

To this end, my lab has two bases, one at the <em>School of Computer Science</em> (Faculty of Science and Engineering) where I affiliate with the [Artificial Intelligence Theme](https://www.cs.manchester.ac.uk/research/themes/artificial-intelligence) and one at the <em>School of Health Sciences</em> where I affiliate with the [Division of Informatics, Imaging and Data Science](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/organisations/division-of-informatics-imaging-data-sciences/) (Faculty of Biology, Medicine and Health) both at the University of Manchester.  

If you are interested, you can check out the work of my <a href="/alumni/">former PhD students</a> and my <a href="/publications/">publications</a>.
